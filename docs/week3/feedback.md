# Week 3: Self-review of personas and wireframes

Reviewed against the James and Donna personas, looking at both the phone and laptop frames of each screen.

## Screen 1: Join queue (James)

> **Works well:** One column, three labeled fields and a full-width "Join queue" button let James see what to do within 5 seconds, even one-handed. The helper text under Email ("Not shown on your ticket view") answers the privacy question at the moment he types, and "Next: your ticket number, position and estimated wait" tells him what happens after he submits. This follows my rule about large, full-width buttons for single-thumb use, and NFR-01.
> **Problem:** The nav shows "Manage queue" (Donna's staff dashboard) and "My place" (meaningless before he has a ticket) to every applicant. On the phone, "Manage queue" even wraps onto two lines. A tired, one-handed James can tap into the wrong screen by accident. This breaks *match with the real world* and *error prevention*: applicants should only see the choices that make sense for them. The "Quick & easy" box also repeats "No account needed" a second time, which pushes the Join button below the fold on a phone.
> **Change:** The applicant nav will show only "Join queue" and "My place" (My place appears once a ticket exists). Staff reach "Manage queue" through a separate staff link, not the applicant nav. I will merge "Takes less than 30 seconds" into the single line under the heading and delete the "Quick & easy" box so the form moves up on the phone.

## Screen 2: My place (James)

> **Works well:** The ticket number is the largest element, the status is a text badge with a dot ("Waiting"), and "Automatic updates on / Last checked 11:42 AM" shows James the information is fresh even on spotty lobby data. This is *visibility of system status*, and nothing relies on sound, which follows my rule about high-contrast badges and auto-refresh in a noisy lobby.
> **Problem:** Two numbers describe the same thing: a large blue "4" (position) and a small "3 people ahead" chip. Beside the ticket number "A-024", a fatigued James can mistake the "4" for his ticket, or read "4" as the number of people ahead. FR-02 asks for the count ahead, and that is the number he uses to decide whether he can step outside with his child. One idea shown as two numbers is a *match with the real world* and *consistency* problem. Also, "Refresh status" is the biggest filled button even though the page updates itself, so it competes with the information, and "Leave the queue" is grey text that looks disabled.
> **Change:** I will make "3 people ahead" the large figure (labelled "People ahead of you") and remove "Your position 4", keeping "≈ 15 min" beside it. "Refresh status" becomes an outlined secondary button, and "Leave the queue" gets darker text and border so it reads as a real button. I will keep the leave confirmation ("Leave this queue?") because it prevents accidental taps. I will also reword my James rule 3 from "exact position in line" to "people ahead" so the rule matches the wireframe.

## Screen 3: Manage queue (Donna)

> **Works well:** "Call next" comes first and names the ticket it will call ("Call next · A-021"), so Donna never has to remember or look up the number. Serve and No-show are attached to the current called ticket (A-020) instead of every table row, so she can't act on the wrong person. Only Waiting and Called tickets appear, with the rest behind the service filter. This follows my rule about a clean table with simple dropdown filters, and her most frequent action is the easiest to reach.
> **Problem:** My rule 2 says Serve should be clear green and No-show outline-red, but "Call next" and "Serve" are both solid blue buttons and "No-show" is a neutral outline. Rushing through the morning peak with reduced mouse accuracy, Donna cannot tell "start the next person" from "finish this person" by color, and No-show (which removes an applicant from the queue) looks as harmless as the button beside it. This is an *error prevention* and *consistency* problem. The grey service and details text in the table is also too light for someone who struggles with small, low-contrast text (my rule 1).
> **Change:** "Serve" becomes solid green, "No-show" a red outline, and "Call next" stays blue so each action has its own color. I will add more space between Serve and No-show and darken the grey table text. I will not add confirmation pop-ups, since Donna's persona dislikes them and the buttons are now distinct.
